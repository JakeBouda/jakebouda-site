***Really, a small thing:***

*Big and small; this and that *— For every point of light visible in the night sky, there are billions both behind and surrounding it, invisible to the naked eye. The space between the earth and the moon is big enough to fit every other planet in the solar system shoulder to shoulder with almost five thousand miles to spare—yet the sun still dwarfs that distance. The Planck length is the smallest unit of measurement humans have postulated yet it is still almost ten billion times smaller than the diameter of a neutron, one of the smallest particles capable of being observed within the confines of light. The number of Planck lengths that span a human brain cell is approximately the same number of brain cells that span the known universe, give or take a few hundred trillion depending on how you’re feeling. The “size” of subatomic particles is only roughly estimated because they cannot be “seen” in the traditional sense; instead, they are measured by how energy is manipulated by their presence—which is like measuring the size of a person by how their voice displaces the air around them in a three-kilometer sphere. To some extent, that’s the same way the size of everything is measured. Quarks are capable of being detected in experiments with particle accelerators that cover miles. The world’s largest particle accelerator has unveiled the smallest known things. When two quantum particles are next to each other, they become *entangled*: a phenomenon wherein it is impossible to describe them independent of one another—how human! After 2,500 years of “extremely productive work” in the field of linguistics, what we have to show for it is that we still have no cohesive idea for what language is, what a word is, what a dialect is or how babies start talking. The twenty-first and twenty-second centuries are presumed to consist of the greatest number of existential risks humankind will ever face because we will have had (or have already had) the greatest number of technologies capable of destroying ourselves yet we will remain incapable of surmounting threats like climate change, asteroid impact or “human factors.” Once humans shed the husk of their own humanness, smooth sailing! *Inflation *refers to the rise in prices that occurs after the value of money decreases—is it so unwise to draw attention to the *cause *of a given phenomenon rather than its *symptoms*? The first word on the United States page in the CIA Global Factbook is “Britain.” *Discreet, *meaning* secretive *or* prudent, *and *Discrete, *meaning* distinct,* can be used to illustrate two opposing states of something. Memetically, humans are beyond categorization or earnest comprehension though the human genome is well-known—22,300 protein-coding genes. One of its findings? the billions of human differences are attributed to 1% of genetic variation—or, rather, 99% genetic singularity.

Just a night… or two.*


*The greatest empire* — Dinosaurs ruled the earth as the dominant terrestrial vertebrate species for over 150 million years. They survived through three geological epochs. First appearing, if you’re being conservative, 233 million years ago and likely dying 66 million years ago to either the Chicxulub impact event or a large volcanic event leading to a runaway global climate disaster that occurred in India—probably both. They declared their independence from and established their global dominance over the Triassic-Jurassic extinction event 201.3 million years ago. Descendants of theirs exist on almost every continent in the world. To our knowledge, they never irrigated their crops, nor established trade routes, nor expanded their empires, nor did they elect a fascist dictator. There is no evidence of their dominant religions, their art or their culture. They consolidated their power primarily in small, feudal, mostly familial kinships where they would vie for the best local resources and available land as these were scarce and they had no way of stratifying or producing more; they provided care for their young but there is no knowledge on whether they did the same for their elders. They were incredibly diverse with not only thousands of races, but over a thousand distinct species. Interspecies relations are presumed to have been fierce and competitive, but there is no data on what interracial relations would have looked like. It is also unknown if they were at all cognizant of race, gender or class. They lived in all known climates—with the exception of the arctic—and they thrived in every known terrain. They held every single one the world's natural resources and precious metals for their entire 150-million-year rule. Inflation was at 0% for the entirety of their existence. Their population remained young with few elders; however, the age ratios are unreliable since there is no census data. Their mortality rate is presumed to have been staggering and in the last days of their empire, it reached 100%—that is to say, there is nothing particularly notable about it. There is no known data on their fertility rate. They had an annual GDP of 0 USD. They lived without sanitation. Their literacy rate is unknown. Their unemployment rate was 100%. They suffered greatly, even mortally, from climate disaster. They had no organized militia. There is no known data on their relationship with capitalism, socialism or communism. It is unknown if they would have complied with or assisted in US interests.

*Legacies* — it can take microseconds to have a thought and milliseconds to speak. The earliest known written word has seen the rise and fall of almost every organized empire in history yet is less than a fiftieth of the age of those who wrote it. The entropy contained in the displacement of air by One’s vocal cords persists for hundreds of billions of years—practically eternity. We are taught to take meaning out of art and treasure it, value it—but does it not just add entropy? is it not just the displacement of air by One’s vocal cords? a thought? The cause of art may be muddled, the implications more so, but are the effects, immediate or ultimate, not always beautiful and infinitely expansive? recurring? recursive? even dialectical?


*What happened when?* — Eta Carinae is a binary-stellar complex and nebula in the milky way galaxy. One of the brightest and most active objects in the night sky, these conjoined stars are an estimated 5 million times more luminous than the sun and 7,500 light years away. They went through what is often referred to as a “great eruption” that began in 1837, the same year that an 18-year-old Queen Victoria acceded the throne and a 25-year-old Charles Dickens published *Oliver Twist*. Over the next 45 years, it fluctuated and oscillated in brightness to a degree barely noticeable to the average observer. This is not the first time this has happened. Later observations have suggested that, because of the surrounding nebulous cloud, the system went through a similar period of change around or during the thirteenth century, the same period that the Kamakura Shogunate established dominance over Japan ending in civil war near the end of the century. Roughly the same time Europe was dying from the plague. Because of the afterbirth of a different star—the last burst of passion of Eta´s parent or parents, the embryo surrounding it, spanning light years, containing buds that will one day bloom into stars and planets after being cast further off into space—the stars are obscured by a pair of glowing orbs, a three-dimensional nephroid. This thing, the Homunculus Nebula, makes it impossible to observe the stars directly; so, they are only observed through the effects they have on the space around them. One or both stars are expected to experience supernovae in the “near astronomical future” (one million years) (a really really long time for any reasonable person). If that happened at this moment, we would be closer in history to the beginning of the series of empires that ruled Egypt than we would be to the point at which we would observe it.

*What are Eta?* — All stars are made up of hydrogen and helium clumped together in such staggering mass that they become harbors for planets; they warp the very fabric of space-time and the two lightest elements in the universe become among the heaviest objects in existence. (Objects? Can they be called objects? They become a form of matter that seems to transcend the very concept of objecthood that such primitive creatures that would go so far as to section off parts of the universe by such a thing as trivial as “objecthood” use to grasp the incomprehensible universe. Is fire an object? Is the flame a different object from the base? The smoke? The heat emanating from it? The translucence that allows one to look through it? The slight stigmatism that forms when the person sitting around the fire stares into the hot coals for too long. No. The star is not separate in its objecthood. Not from the elements ferociously burning in its core, nor the corona surrounding the moon’s surface during an eclipse, nor the heat and photons that speed through space to turn to thoughts in One’s eyes as they observe from earth. Though a human may be traced by their skin, their voice may still touch everything around them.) The hydrogen and helium in the star undergo changes—they become each other; they drift outward and sink back inward, an elemental stew that brews and circulates in the star’s mantle, churning, turning into carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, neon, magnesium and, finally, iron where they smolder like the hot coals at the bottom of the fire pit; gases die in metal, liquid, gaseous plasma-metal; meanwhile, the star is still burning yet beginning to die, diminish. The cross a precipice. Hydrogen and helium are easy to fuse; they bind easily; they require relatively little mass and energy to fuse which, itself, is a process of creating energy which, itself, is a process of creating matter. As time goes on and they run out of H and He to fuse together, they turn to the later, heavier, elements and continue until they become so massive and the fuel they use to counterbalance the force of their own gravity diminishes. They ultimately collapse in on themselves—some sort of paradox of temporal and volumetric folding and unfolding, unfathomable and nonsensical phenomena, Tuesday for the universe, existence for human beings, fate for all… Every star starts as a blank slate, unknown with regard to which subgroup (gender) (genre) (type) they will inhabit. The conditions handed to them at their conception are crucial in determining which stratification they will remain in for the rest of their lives. A stellar caste casted in a mold of stellar ash. Eta were born with such excesses in early-formation mass that two blue giants were formed, stars that are so bloated in mass and energy that they spin incomprehensibly fast and shine shamelessly. They are so large that the process of stellar collapse is quickened by a factor of over 1,000. They are another statistic in infant mortality rates. Their staggering mass causes the cores to be far hotter than any normal, reasonable star. They are capitalists hell-bent on limitless expansion that they burn all their profits on acquisitions. Instead of living in their main sequence stage for billions of years, they remain there for a mere million or two—still long enough for the evolutionary tribe Hominini to split from the rest of the great apes. While making their lifespan shorter, this also makes them burn far brighter than any other class of star. Anyone who’s peered into the Homunculus that imprisons them knows this: they chose the stereotypical life of the deeply troubled yet brilliant artist! Really, they are a small thing.

*Aging* — It is impossible to know how old or even how massive the stars are. *Redshift — *At this point, we have several telescopes floating around in space—projects that cost about as many dollars as there are brain cells in the average human’s dorsolateral and ventromedial prefrontal cortices, which are meant to unveil the deepest secrets of the universe. The newest and most impressive one, the James Webb Space Telescope, is a marvel of modern photographic technology. Its lens is made up of an array of gold-plated hexagonal mirrors adding up to a lens area of over 26 m2. Its main function is to collect infrared and near-infrared images of the early and distant universe which allows us to look at ancient, deep-space objects with truly magical clarity. Infrared imaging is crucial in this because of the red-shifting effect of distant objects—that is, the relativistic visual phenomenon similar to the Doppler effect wherein objects that are moving away from a given focal point drop in photon energy, and thus electromagnetic frequency, causing them to appear more red. Conversely, approaching objects blueshift and appear slightly blue as they approach. The Doppler effect is this same process but with sound in that the frequency and relative pitch of a sound rises as it approaches and drops as it moves away, commonly observed with an approaching and receding car. The James Webb Space Telescope, with its massive array of gold-plated mirrors, can capture deep space objects at staggering definition and depth. A single grain of sand held at arm’s length becomes the entire visual space it plays with, each of those 26 square meters focused on that single grain of sand, sparkling, twinkling in starlight. *I look out to this field—each one a field in itself, its essence, its imperceptibility. *Entanglement *— Pronouns are morphemes that refer to nouns—they are in a state of entanglement. The best analogy I’ve heard for them is that they’re “like pointers in code.” When One is writing verbal code (speaking, often thinking), instead of spelling out the entirety of another person’s name, for example, they “invoke the pointer” labeled in their lexicon (memory banks, memory) as “he,” “she,” “they,” “we,” “I,” “you” and “it.” They are a tool that makes talking less repetitive, less dry, more *flavorful*. This flavor is often regarded in languages that have a grammatical gender because gender (genre) adds more categorization saying, “this is what kind of thing this is! This is its flavor!” Which is to say they are of a certain morphological shape. Minds like for there to be categories, for there to be a distinct border between things, attitudes, memories, hopes, people. It helps us learn, to make a model of the world that does a better job at being more reflective of reality than before. It helps us remember. Human experience is never disentangled from memory. Even when human beings don’t remember, they are forming memories that can either be explicit (remembered) or implicit (habitual, learned). There is *discrete memory* which is contrasted with *discreet memory*. Memory terraforms experience—it gives it flavor, color, even rhythm and idiom. Memories, by virtue of principle, are never disentangled from time. Time is entangled with experience, memories, hopes, observations, ideas, thoughts, space and stories. When humans use pronouns, they differentiate which pronoun is which by placing them in temporal proximity to that with which they are entangled. Entanglement is a confused mess. Syntax is entangled with time which is entangled with experience which is entangled with memory which is entangled with time which is etc.—how human! It would seem that one of the cornerstones of human intelligence, obvious in language, is the ability to be endlessly recursive—to make the first step for building a stone tool:
